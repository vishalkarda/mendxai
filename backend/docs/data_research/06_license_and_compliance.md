# License and Compliance

This file summarizes what the MODMA dataset's user license agreement actually permits and requires, in prose, and flags one real compliance gap worth knowing about before sharing anything derived from this data outside the project. The source document is `backend/data/MODMADatasetUserLicenseAgreement (1).docx` — a signed copy of the agreement, not a blank template, submitted for a project described as a voice-based early-detection system for mental health disorders. That docx is gitignored along with the rest of `backend/data/`, so treat this file — not the docx — as the place to look up license terms during day-to-day work.

## What the license actually restricts

The core restriction is narrow and unambiguous: the dataset may only be used for academic research, never for commercial purposes. The agreement is specific about what counts as commercial use, explicitly naming four things: training or demonstrating the efficiency of a commercial system, using participant screenshots or likenesses in advertising, selling any portion of the dataset, or building military applications. If this project, or any derivative of it, ever moves toward a commercial product, this dataset (and anything trained directly on it) cannot come along without separately renegotiating access.

Distribution is even more tightly restricted than commercial use. The default rule is that you may not distribute the dataset, or any portion of it, in any way. There is exactly one carve-out: small portions of data may be used in academic publications or presentations, specifically for the purpose of clarifying or illustrating that publication — think a short audio clip or a small feature snippet used as a figure, not a data release. Even that narrow exception comes with a further condition that's easy to miss: it only applies to *subjects who explicitly consented* to having their physiological data used in publications and presentations, and the agreement states that consent information for each participant is recorded in "the participant questionnaire file." That phrasing matters for this project specifically — see the compliance gap below.

Access to the dataset itself is gated: obtaining it requires printing, signing, and returning both the EULA and a separate User Information Form to the MODMA administrators, after which access is granted per-account through the MODMA project's own website. Account credentials are meant to stay with the person who requested access — not shared onward. Administrators reserve the right to amend the agreement's terms over time; users are notified before changes take effect and may opt out, but opting out means losing continued access to the dataset. The dataset itself is provided as-is, with the administrators explicitly disclaiming liability for any physical, financial, or other damage arising from its use.

## The citation requirement

Any publication or presentation that uses this data must credit "Gansu Provincial Key Laboratory of Wearable Computing, Lanzhou University, China" as the data source and cite the dataset paper directly:

> Cai, H., Gao, Y., Sun, S., Li, N., Tian, F., Xiao, H., Li, J., Yang, Z., Li, X., Zhao, Q., Liu, Z., Yao, Z., Yang, M., Peng, H., Zhu, J., Zhang, X., Hu, X., & Hu, B. (2020). MODMA dataset: a Multi-modal Open Dataset for Mental-disorder Analysis. arXiv preprint arXiv:2002.09283.

This is a low-cost, easy-to-satisfy requirement — just make sure any write-up, slide deck, or report that shows results from this dataset includes that citation.

## A real compliance gap worth flagging

The narrow publication exception described above only applies to subjects who explicitly consented to their data being used that way — and that consent is supposed to be recorded per-subject somewhere. The metadata schema actually documented and used throughout this project (see `02_metadata_schema_deep_dive.md`) has exactly 11 real data columns: subject id, type, age, gender, education, and the six clinical scales. None of them is a consent flag. The two columns dropped as "not data" during loading (`Unnamed: 11`, mostly blank, and `Unnamed: 12`, which holds an abbreviation legend and a general remark about subject-ID consistency) were re-checked specifically for this — neither contains a per-subject consent indicator either.

In practical terms: before including any subject-level audio excerpt, waveform image, or other identifiable derived artifact in a paper, presentation, or shared report, there is currently no confirmed source in this repository's data for which subjects consented to that kind of use. The safest working assumption until this is resolved is to treat *no* subject as pre-cleared for that narrow publication exception, and to only share aggregate, non-attributable statistics (the kind already used throughout `04_eda_findings_and_statistics.md` — group means, effect sizes, counts) rather than anything traceable to an individual subject's recording. If a consent field does exist somewhere in the original MODMA data release outside what's currently stored in this repository's copy of the metadata workbook, that would be worth tracking down before any subject-specific material is shared externally.
